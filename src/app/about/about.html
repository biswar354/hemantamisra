A brief biography

Hemanta Misra (1917–2009)

Hemanta Misra was born in Sivasagar, Assam, on 13 October 1917. He studied in Sivasagar and Guwahati, and later attended Cotton College and St Edmund’s College in Shillong. He was married to Bina Misra, a teacher and writer.

Primarily self-taught, he also studied art by correspondence with British artist John Hassall. Drawing and sketching gave him a strong foundation as his work moved from landscapes into cubist experiments and, later, a distinctive surrealism shaped by mysticism and poetry.

In 1953 he became a member of the Calcutta Group, a collective associated with a new modernist direction in Indian art. His paintings and writing helped bring Assam’s artistic voice to a wider national audience.

Misra also wrote poetry and books in Assamese, including Bharatiya Chitrakala and the autobiographical work Dikhow Luit Aru Sagar. He passed away in Guwahati on 31 December 2009.
